MACHINE LEARNING

ALGORITHM

LEARNING OUTCOMES

1: Articulate the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues faced by machine learning professionals.
2: Understand the applicability and challenges associated with different datasets for the use of machine learning algorithms.
3: Apply and critically appraise machine learning techniques to real-world problems, particularly where technical risk and uncertainty is involved.
4: Systematically develop and implement the skills required to be effective member of a development team in a virtual professional environment, adopting real-life perspectives on team roles and organisation.

REFLECTIONS

Unit 1
This project deepened my understanding of how Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0 shape modern healthcare, particularly through machine learning and digital integration. While I initially focused on technical vulnerabilities, peer feedback and further reading helped me recognise that cybersecurity failures often stem from governance, oversight, and organisational behaviour. This realisation shifted my perspective toward Industry 5.0’s human‑centred resilience, where the priority is maintaining safe, continuous patient care even during digital disruption. Through collaborative discussion, critical self‑analysis, and engagement with literature, I developed stronger analytical skills, a more nuanced understanding of ethical and professional responsibilities, and a clearer appreciation of how machine learning practitioners must balance innovation with robust security and governance. My contributions and reflections demonstrate meaningful learning that I can apply to real‑world data science and healthcare contexts.
Reflecting on the discussion of the 4th Industrial Revolution, I am increasingly aware of how deeply machine learning and digital technologies are reshaping healthcare—and how unprepared many organisations still are for the risks that accompany this transformation. The WannaCry attack on the NHS was a reminder that technological progress can quickly become a liability when governance, patching, and oversight fall behind. Peer insights on network segmentation, secure backups, and zero‑trust approaches made me think more critically about the practical skills and responsibilities required of anyone working with machine learning systems. Géron’s introduction reinforced this by showing that machine learning is not only about building models but understanding their limitations, vulnerabilities, and ethical implications. This has made me reflect on my own development: becoming proficient in machine learning means learning to anticipate failure, design for resilience, and ensure that intelligent systems ultimately protect people rather than expose them to harm.

Unit 2
Exploratory data analysis on the auto‑mpg dataset strengthened my ability to understand and prepare real‑world data for machine learning. Identifying missing values, examining skewness and kurtosis, and interpreting correlation and scatter‑plot relationships helped me recognise how data quality and distribution shape model behaviour. The strong negative correlation between mpg and weight, along with visible patterns in the pair plot, improved my understanding of feature interactions and their implications for predictive modelling. Converting categorical values to numerical form further prepared the dataset for algorithmic use. This work also highlighted ethical responsibilities around data accuracy, transparency, and bias when handling real‑world datasets. Collaborating in a virtual team environment enhanced my communication and problem‑solving skills, reinforcing the importance of shared understanding during technical tasks. Overall, Unit 2 deepened my confidence in applying EDA techniques and critically evaluating dataset suitability under conditions of uncertainty.

Unit 3
Unit 3 strengthened my theoretical and practical understanding of correlation and regression by applying these techniques to real datasets. Computing covariance and Pearson’s correlation helped me interpret relationships between variables, such as the strong positive correlation (0.979) observed in the covariance_pearson_correlation exercise. Implementing simple linear regression allowed me to predict values, including estimating y = 85.59 when x = 10, which demonstrated how regression models support real‑world decision‑making. Multiple linear regression further expanded my skills by incorporating several predictors, as shown in the CO₂ prediction example. Polynomial regression highlighted how non‑linear patterns can be modelled effectively, reinforcing the importance of selecting appropriate techniques for different datasets. These activities improved my confidence in analysing data, interpreting statistical outputs, and understanding how machine learning models behave under uncertainty. Overall, Unit 3 deepened my ability to apply correlation and regression meaningfully in practical scenarios.
